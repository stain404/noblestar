Whether the delivery is to a manufacturing site or the end customer, Noble Star road
freight is smooth, efficient and sustainable. Road is the workhorse of intra-GCC trade —
faster than sea on short lanes, far cheaper than air, and door to door without a terminal
in the middle.

## What the service includes

- **Full-truck load (FTL)** — dedicated vehicle, direct routing, fastest transit
- **Shared load (LTL)** — scheduled departures on the main lanes, priced by space used
- **First and last mile** — collection and delivery within the UAE using our own drivers
- **Border management** — customs declarations, conformity certificates and transit
  paperwork prepared before the truck leaves

## Equipment

Curtain-side and box trailers for general cargo, flatbeds and lowbeds for machinery and
out-of-gauge loads, and temperature-controlled reefers for food and pharmaceutical
consignments. Tell us the commodity, weight and dimensions and we will specify the right
vehicle rather than the most convenient one.

## Why border preparation matters

Most cross-border delays in the GCC are documentation problems, not traffic. Saudi
Arabia's SABER conformity requirement in particular catches out shippers who have not
registered their products before dispatch. We check conformity, origin and permit
requirements at the booking stage, so problems surface in our office and not at Al Batha
with a loaded truck waiting.

## The borders, one by one

**Saudi Arabia — Ghuwaifat / Al Batha.** The busiest commercial crossing between the UAE
and Saudi Arabia, and the gateway for almost all Dubai–Riyadh and Dubai–Jeddah road
freight. Crossings are routine when the file is complete and slow when it is not. This
is also the crossing through which Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain road traffic passes, since
all three route overland through Saudi territory.

**Oman — Hatta / Wajajah, Khatmat Malaha, Mezyad.** Short crossings with generally quick
formalities, helped by GCC common-customs treatment for goods already cleared into the
UAE. Hatta/Wajajah serves Muscat and the north, Khatmat Malaha serves the Batinah coast,
and Mezyad connects from Al Ain. Dubai to Muscat is commonly a one to two day door-to-door
movement.

**Qatar — Abu Samra, via Saudi Arabia.** Road movements into Qatar transit Saudi
territory, so the consignment needs both Saudi transit documentation and Qatari import
paperwork. Where transit permits are constrained, short-sea from Jebel Ali to Hamad Port
is often the more predictable option and we will say so.

**Kuwait — Nuwaiseeb, via Saudi Arabia.** A long haul, typically four to five days from
Dubai, crossing the full length of Saudi Arabia. The consignee's import licence must be
valid before arrival.

**Bahrain — King Fahd Causeway, via Saudi Arabia.** Cargo crosses into Saudi Arabia and
then over the causeway. Goods already cleared into the GCC customs union may move on a
statistical declaration rather than a full import entry.

## SABER, SASO and FASAH, in plain terms

Three Saudi requirements account for the majority of border problems we see, and they are
frequently confused with one another.

**SASO** is the Saudi Standards Organisation. Its conformity requirements decide whether
a product may legally be sold in the Kingdom.

**SABER** is the online platform through which conformity is registered. A product needs
a Product Certificate of Conformity, and then each shipment needs a Shipment Certificate
of Conformity issued against it. The product registration is the slow part and it is done
once; shippers who leave it until the truck is loaded discover that it cannot be
completed in a day.

**FASAH** is the Saudi single-window platform through which the manifest and customs
declaration are filed. It is a filing step rather than a certification step, but it has
to be done in advance of arrival.

The practical consequence is simple: conformity registration belongs at the purchase
order stage, not the dispatch stage. We check where a product stands before quoting a
lane into Saudi Arabia, because the honest answer to "can this truck leave on Thursday"
sometimes depends on a registration nobody has started.

## FTL or shared load

A full truck is the right answer when you have the volume to fill one, when transit time
matters, or when the cargo should not be handled alongside other shippers' goods. It goes
direct, it is sealed once, and it is the fastest road option on every lane we run.

A shared load is the right answer for part-volumes. You pay for the space you occupy, and
scheduled departures on the main Dubai–Saudi and Dubai–Oman lanes mean you are rarely
waiting long for one. The trade-off is consolidation and deconsolidation time at each
end, which typically adds a day or two.

Where the two are close on price, we will quote both and tell you which we would choose.

## What delays a truck

1. **Missing conformity certification** for the destination market — by a wide margin the
   most common cause on the Saudi lane.
2. **Invoice and packing list that disagree** with each other or with the declaration.
3. **Consignee documentation** not in place: an expired trade licence or, for Kuwait, an
   import licence that has lapsed.
4. **Peak congestion** around public holidays and the end of the calendar year, when
   commercial crossings queue.

The first three are preventable in our office. We would rather delay a departure by a day
to fix paperwork than send a loaded truck to a border where it will sit.
