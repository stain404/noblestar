Air freight shipments are treated as our top priority. When a consignment is going by
air, something downstream is usually waiting on it — a production line, a tender
deadline, a vessel sailing. We make sure every kilo air-freighted reaches its
destination safely and on time.

## What the service includes

- **Space booking** across scheduled carriers out of Dubai International (DXB), Al
  Maktoum (DWC), Abu Dhabi (AUH) and Sharjah (SHJ)
- **Export and import customs clearance** filed in-house
- **Pickup and delivery** through our own driver fleet in the UAE and vetted partners
  elsewhere in the GCC
- **Charter and on-board courier** arrangements for genuinely critical cargo

## When air freight is the right call

Air is the right answer for high-value, low-volume, perishable or urgent cargo. It is
rarely the right answer for heavy, low-value goods, where the freight cost can exceed
the value of the shipment. If you are weighing air against sea on a lane where transit
time is not critical, ask us for both — we would rather quote you honestly than book the
more expensive mode.

## Chargeable weight, and why quotes surprise people

Air freight is not priced on what your cargo weighs. It is priced on **chargeable
weight**, which is whichever is greater: the actual gross weight, or the volumetric
weight calculated as length × width × height in centimetres divided by 6000.

A pallet of cushions measuring 120 × 100 × 150 cm weighs perhaps 80 kg on the scale. Its
volumetric weight is 1,800,000 ÷ 6000 = 300 kg. It will be charged as 300 kg. A pallet of
machine parts the same size might weigh 700 kg and be charged as 700 kg.

This is the single most common reason an air quote comes back higher than expected, and
it is why we ask for dimensions rather than weight alone. If your cargo is light and
bulky, it is worth checking whether it can be packed more densely before booking — the
saving is often larger than any rate negotiation.

## The airports we work through

Dubai International (DXB) has the widest belly-hold capacity in the region because of the
sheer number of passenger flights, which makes it the default for general cargo on most
lanes. Al Maktoum (DWC) is the freighter airport and is usually the answer for outsized
consignments, livestock, and anything needing a main-deck loader. Abu Dhabi (AUH) and
Sharjah (SHJ) both carry useful freighter capacity and are sometimes materially cheaper
on specific routes.

Within the GCC we routinely uplift into Riyadh (RUH), Jeddah (JED), Dammam (DMM), Hamad
International (DOH), Kuwait (KWI) and Bahrain (BAH). Most of these are one to two days
door to door from Dubai when documentation is ready.

## Special handling

We arrange temperature-controlled uplift for pharmaceuticals and perishables, including
active and passive cold-chain packaging, and we handle valuable cargo under airline
secure procedures. For food consignments, see our
[food cargo service](/services/food-cargo).

Temperature-controlled air freight needs earlier booking than general cargo. Active
containers are a limited resource at every airport, and passive solutions have a defined
duration that has to cover the whole journey, not just the flight — including the hours
the shipment spends on the ground at each end. Tell us the required temperature range and
the maximum permissible excursion at the quoting stage.

## Dangerous goods

Dangerous goods move by air routinely, but they move under IATA rules that decide
packaging, labelling, documentation and which aircraft may carry them. Some classes are
forbidden on passenger aircraft entirely and must go on a freighter, which changes both
the routing and the price.

We need the material safety data sheet and the UN number before we can quote — not after
booking. A shipment tendered as general cargo that turns out to be dangerous goods will
be rejected at acceptance, and the cost of that is a missed flight plus repacking.

## What delays air freight

Air moves fast enough that a single day lost matters. In practice the causes are:

1. **Documentation not ready at acceptance.** The air waybill, invoice and packing list
   have to be complete when the cargo is tendered, not in progress.
2. **Capacity on the lane.** Belly-hold space contracts when passenger schedules thin out,
   and freighter capacity on GCC lanes tightens around peak retail seasons.
3. **Security screening.** Certain commodities require additional screening or a known
   consignor status. This is predictable, and we plan for it, but it is not instant.
4. **Undeclared dangerous goods.** Discovered at acceptance, this stops everything.

## Getting a quote

Send us the origin and destination airports or door addresses, the gross weight, the
dimensions of every piece, the commodity, and the deadline you are working to. If the
cargo is temperature-controlled or hazardous, say so at this stage. We will come back
with the earliest realistic uplift rather than the most optimistic one — and if sea or
road would meet your deadline at a fraction of the cost, we will tell you that too.
