Noble Star Shipping offers smooth connectivity for LCL cargo across major shipping routes
throughout the GCC. Less than container load consolidation lets you ship modest volumes
at sea freight economics — you buy space in a shared container instead of paying for a
box you cannot fill.

## Who LCL suits

LCL is the right choice when you are moving somewhere between one and roughly thirteen
cubic metres. Below one cubic metre, courier or air freight is often simpler and not much
more expensive. Above thirteen, a 20ft container usually costs less and moves faster.

## What the service includes

- **Consolidation** at origin, with your cargo received, checked and manifested
- **Ocean leg** on scheduled sailings with our co-loading partners
- **Deconsolidation** at destination
- **Customs clearance** filed in-house
- **Final delivery** to the consignee's door

## Understanding revenue tonne

LCL is priced on whichever is greater: the cargo's volume in cubic metres, or its weight
in tonnes. Three cubic metres weighing one tonne is charged as three revenue tonnes. One
cubic metre weighing three tonnes is also charged as three. This catches out first-time
shippers of dense goods — send us both figures and we will give you the real number
rather than an optimistic one.

### Three worked examples

**Six pallets of packaging material.** 8 m³, 1.2 tonnes. Volume wins: charged as 8
revenue tonnes. Light and bulky cargo is always charged on the space it occupies.

**Two pallets of ceramic tiles.** 2.4 m³, 3.8 tonnes. Weight wins: charged as 3.8 revenue
tonnes despite occupying very little space. Shippers of dense goods who budget on volume
alone are consistently surprised here.

**One pallet of spare parts.** 0.6 m³, 0.4 tonnes. Neither reaches the one revenue tonne
minimum, so the shipment is charged at the minimum. Below roughly one cubic metre, check
whether air freight or a courier is genuinely more expensive — often it is not, and it is
considerably faster.

## The consolidation timeline

The transit times quoted above include consolidation, and that is deliberate — quoting
port-to-port sailing time for an LCL shipment gives a number nobody actually experiences.

A typical LCL movement runs: cargo received into the origin warehouse, held while the
consolidation fills, stuffed and manifested, the ocean leg itself, then deconsolidation at
destination before customs clearance can begin on your portion. The two consolidation
steps commonly add three to five days at each end.

This is the real reason LCL is slower than FCL on the same lane, and it is worth planning
around rather than discovering. If your cargo is ready the day after a consolidation
closes, it waits for the next one. Tell us your ready date early and we will tell you
which departure it realistically makes.

## When to switch to FCL

The crossover point sits around 13 to 15 cubic metres on most lanes, but it moves with the
rate environment, and it moves earlier than people expect when any of the following apply:

- **Your cargo is fragile.** LCL cargo is handled at consolidation, at the port, at
  deconsolidation and again at delivery. FCL is sealed once.
- **Your deadline is tight.** The consolidation steps are not compressible.
- **Your goods are high-value.** Sharing a container means sharing it with cargo you have
  not inspected, and claims are more complicated when a container holds several shippers'
  goods.
- **You ship the same lane regularly.** Consistent volume is often better served by a
  scheduled FCL programme than by repeated LCL bookings.

We will price both whenever it is close, and say which we would choose in your position.

## Packing advice

Palletise where you can, shrink-wrap, and mark every piece clearly with the consignee and
your reference. LCL cargo is handled at consolidation, at the port, at deconsolidation
and at delivery. Packaging that survives an FCL move may not survive an LCL one.

A few specifics that reduce damage claims materially: keep pallets within standard
footprints so they stack and handle predictably; do not overhang the pallet edge, because
overhanging cartons are the first thing crushed; band or shrink-wrap to the pallet rather
than relying on the cartons' own strength; and mark fragile or this-way-up on every face
rather than one. Shipping marks should identify the consignee without needing the
paperwork, since at deconsolidation your cargo is separated from several other shippers'
goods by eye.

## Insurance on shared containers

Carrier liability on an ocean movement is limited by convention and is calculated per
package or per kilo, not on what the goods are worth. For most cargo that limit is well
below the commercial value, and it applies whether the container was yours alone or
shared.

That gap matters more on LCL than on FCL, simply because the cargo is handled more often
and by more parties. Marine cargo insurance covers the difference, and it is inexpensive
relative to the value at risk. We can arrange it as part of the booking or work alongside
a policy you already hold — but either way, tell us the commercial value of the
consignment rather than the freight cost, because those are the numbers that get confused
when a claim is actually made.

## What we need to quote

Origin and destination, the commodity, the total volume in cubic metres, the total gross
weight, the number and type of pieces, and the date the cargo will be ready. Both the
volume and the weight matter here rather than one or the other, because the revenue tonne
calculation needs both. Tell us if anything is fragile, stackable or temperature
sensitive, and we will say plainly whether LCL is the right answer or whether a 20ft
container would serve you better.
