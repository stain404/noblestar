Most customs delays are not caused by customs. They are caused by paperwork that does not
agree with itself. Below is what Dubai Customs actually expects on a standard import, and
the specific mismatches that turn a one-day clearance into a one-week one.

## The core document set

Every import declaration needs these five, without exception:

1. **Commercial invoice** — showing the seller, the buyer, a full description of the
   goods, unit and total values, the currency, and the agreed incoterm.
2. **Packing list** — itemising each package with gross and net weights and dimensions.
3. **Bill of lading or air waybill** — the transport document issued by the carrier.
4. **Certificate of origin** — issued by the chamber of commerce in the country of
   export, and attested where the goods qualify for preferential duty.
5. **Importer credentials** — a valid trade licence and customs importer code for the
   consignee.

## The documents people forget

Beyond the core five, regulated commodities carry their own requirements. Food needs
municipality registration and an approved Arabic label. Cosmetics and health products
need ministry registration. Telecom and wireless equipment needs TDRA approval. Medical
devices need MOHAP registration.

The pattern is consistent: these approvals are obtained *before* the goods arrive. A
consignment that lands without them sits in the port accruing storage while the
registration runs its course — which can take weeks.

## Why consignments actually get held

In our day-to-day work, holds cluster around three causes:

**Documents that contradict each other.** The invoice says 480 kg, the packing list says
520 kg, the bill of lading says 500 kg. Any two documents disagreeing on value, weight,
quantity or description is enough to trigger a query. This is the single most common
problem we see, and it is entirely preventable by checking the set as a set before filing.

**An incorrect HS code.** The HS code determines the duty rate and whether the goods are
restricted. An optimistic or careless classification changes the duty payable and can
trigger a physical inspection. Getting the classification right the first time is
cheaper than arguing about it later.

**Undervaluation.** Customs maintains reference values. An invoice priced well below the
expected range for that commodity invites a valuation query, and you will be asked to
evidence the price actually paid.

## A realistic timeline

With a complete, internally consistent file, a standard Dubai import clears in **24 to 36
business hours**. Add two to four business days if the consignment is selected for
physical inspection, and longer if a ministry or municipality approval is outstanding.

Anyone promising you same-day clearance as a matter of routine is describing the best
case, not the normal one.

## Practical advice

Send your forwarder the document set *before* the goods ship, not when they arrive. A
ten-minute review at that stage catches the weight mismatch, the missing attestation and
the questionable HS code while they are still cheap to fix. Once the container is on the
water, your options narrow considerably.
