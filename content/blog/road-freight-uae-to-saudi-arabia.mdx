The UAE to Saudi Arabia lane is the busiest cross-border road corridor in the GCC, and
the one where preparation makes the biggest difference. A well-prepared truck crosses at
Al Batha in hours. A poorly prepared one sits there for days.

## Transit times

From Dubai, realistic door-to-door times are:

- **Riyadh** — 2 to 3 days
- **Dammam** — 1 to 2 days
- **Jeddah** — 3 to 4 days

These assume a full-truck load with documentation in order. Shared-load departures add a
day or two for consolidation. Ramadan, Hajj season and the run-up to major holidays all
add congestion at the border.

## Which border crossing

Two crossings matter on this lane:

**Al Batha** is the main commercial crossing, connecting Abu Dhabi to the Eastern Province
and onward to Riyadh. It handles the bulk of freight and has the infrastructure to match.

**Ghuwaifat**, on the UAE side, is the corresponding post. Traffic is heavy and processing
times vary considerably with the time of day and the season.

For cargo destined for Jeddah or the west, routing is still via the Eastern Province —
there is no shorter road option from the UAE.

## SABER: the thing that stops trucks

If your goods are subject to Saudi technical regulation — and a wide range of consumer
and industrial products are — they need a **SABER** product certificate and a shipment
certificate before arrival. SABER is the online platform through which SASO conformity is
demonstrated.

The process has two stages. First the product is registered and a product certificate
obtained, which is a one-time exercise per product per certification body. Then a shipment
certificate is raised against each consignment. The first stage takes time. The second is
quick, *provided the first is already done*.

This is the single most common cause of multi-day border delays we see. A truck arrives,
the shipment certificate cannot be raised because the product was never registered, and
the cargo waits while the registration runs. There is no way to shortcut it at the border.

## FASAH

Alongside SABER, the manifest must be filed electronically through **FASAH**, Saudi
Arabia's single-window trade platform, before arrival. Late or inaccurate filing causes
its own delays, and it is straightforward to get right if it is done in advance.

## The document set

For a standard commercial movement you will need:

1. Commercial invoice and packing list
2. Certificate of origin, attested
3. SABER product and shipment certificates for regulated goods
4. FASAH manifest filed before arrival
5. Customs declaration or GCC statistical declaration
6. Driver and vehicle transit documentation

## Practical advice

Register your products in SABER before you have cargo to move, not when a truck is
loading. Registration is a per-product exercise with a lead time; once done, subsequent
shipments of the same product are quick.

And check the certificate scope covers what you are actually shipping. A product
certificate that covers one model but not the variant on the truck is, at the border,
the same as having no certificate at all.
